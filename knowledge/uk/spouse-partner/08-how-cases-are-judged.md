# How Cases Are Judged — case by case

Two decision-makers, two different mindsets. Review every file through **both lenses**.

## Lens 1 — Home Office caseworker (on the papers)
Caseworkers work fast through a checklist. They rarely ask for more evidence (though FM-SE para D
"evidential flexibility" allows requests for missing items in a sequence/minor defects — don't rely on it).

```
Valid application? ─► Suitability (Part Suitability) ─► Eligibility, in order:
  relationship → financial (FM-SE strict!) → accommodation → English → immigration status (in-UK)
      │
      ├─ all met ─────────────────────────────► GRANT (5-year route)
      └─ any not met ─► EX.1 (in-UK only)? ─► GEN.3.1/3.2 exceptional circumstances? ─► 10-yr grant or REFUSE
```
**What makes a caseworker refuse:** a missing specified document; a number that doesn't reconcile
(payslip ≠ bank credit); a date outside the window; an unexplained inconsistency; thin relationship
evidence with a red flag; anything suggesting deception.
**What makes a caseworker grant easily:** an indexed bundle, a cover letter mapping every requirement
to evidence, explanations for every oddity *before* they find it.

## Lens 2 — First-tier Tribunal judge (appeal)
The only ground for most partner appeals: **refusal is unlawful under s.6 HRA 1998 (Art 8)**.
1. Judge finds facts (balance of probabilities) — oral evidence from both partners matters.
2. **Are the rules met at the date of hearing?** If yes → normally allow (*TZ (Pakistan)*).
   (New evidence can be considered, but a "new matter" needs SSHD consent — s.85(5) NIAA 2002.)
3. If not met → Razgar 5 steps; proportionality balance sheet with **s.117B** mandatory factors.
4. Children's best interests first (*Zoumbas*); EX.1/insurmountable obstacles (*Agyarko*, *Lal*);
   unjustifiably harsh consequences (GEN.3.2); Chikwamba where relevant (*Alam*).
5. Credibility: consistency between statements, documents and oral evidence; plausibility; explanation of red flags.

**Outcome guide:** all-rules-met + no negatives → strong grant. Rules unmet + only "hardship/preference"
→ refusal likely, appeal weak. Rules unmet + British child or evidenced serious obstacle → arguable,
needs lawyer and expert evidence.

## Burden & standard
- Applicant proves facts on **balance of probabilities**.
- SSHD bears burden for deception/sham allegations (*AA (Nigeria)*, *Agho*, *Rosa*).
- Date of assessment: caseworker — date of decision; tribunal — date of hearing (human-rights appeals).
