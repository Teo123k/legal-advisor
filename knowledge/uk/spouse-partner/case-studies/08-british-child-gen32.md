# 08 — Income too low, British child (GEN.3.1 / GEN.3.2)

**Facts:** British sponsor (part-time, £17,000) in UK with their 3-year-old British child. Applicant
(the child's other parent, Ghanaian) in Ghana. No savings. Sponsor's parents (homeowners, retired,
£40,000 pension income) willing to provide £600/month and accommodation. Applicant is a qualified nurse
with a conditional UK job offer.

**Issues:** MIR not met; third-party support & prospective earnings don't count under the rules → GEN.3.1;
unjustifiably harsh consequences → GEN.3.2; best interests of the British child (s.55, GEN.3.3).

**Analysis**
- *MM (Lebanon)*: decision-maker must consider credible third-party support and prospective earnings where
  refusal would be unjustifiably harsh.
- Child's best interests (primary consideration, *Zoumbas*): being raised by both parents; child cannot be
  expected to relocate realistically? (British child, *KO (Nigeria)* reasonableness framing).
- Credibility evidence: grandparents' finances (pension statements, bank statements), signed undertaking,
  history of support; applicant's NMC registration progress, job offer letter.

**Strategy:** Regulated adviser; detailed representations; independent social worker report optional.
Expect a 10-year route grant if successful; appeal likely if refused.

**Verdict:** 🔴 (arguable) — depends on evidence quality.
