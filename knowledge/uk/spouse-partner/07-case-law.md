# Case Law Digest — UK Spouse / Partner & Article 8

> **Status key:** `core` = landmark, widely cited, citation well-established · `verify` = check judgment
> text before quoting to the Home Office/tribunal (AGENTS.md 1.5). Update status to
> `verified (YYYY-MM-DD)` after checking on caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk / BAILII / UTIAC.
> **Hierarchy:** UKSC/HL > Court of Appeal > reported Upper Tribunal. Unreported UT/FtT decisions are not precedent.

## A. Financial requirement

### MM (Lebanon) & Ors v SSHD [2017] UKSC 10 — `core`
- **Principle:** The minimum income requirement is lawful in principle, but the rules/guidance unlawfully
  failed to (i) treat children's best interests properly and (ii) allow alternative sources (third-party
  support, prospective earnings) to be considered where Art 8 requires.
- **Effect:** Led to **GEN.3.1–3.3** (Aug 2017).
- **How lawyers use it:** Where the MIR isn't met but there is reliable third-party support or the applicant
  has strong job prospects, run GEN.3.1 with evidence of credibility and sustainability; foreground children.

### Balajigari & Ors v SSHD [2019] EWCA Civ 673 — `core`
- **Principle:** Where the Home Office suspects dishonesty from discrepancies between earnings declared to
  HMRC and to the Home Office, procedural fairness requires the applicant be told and given a chance to explain.
- **Use:** Self-employed sponsors with amended tax returns; argue fairness; but better — explain amendments
  up-front with accountant's letter.

### Rhuppiah v SSHD [2018] UKSC 58 — `core`
- **Principle:** "Financially independent" in s.117B(3) means not financially dependent on the **state**;
  "precarious" (s.117B(5)) covers any leave short of ILR.

## B. Relationship (genuine & subsisting / sham)

### GA ("Subsisting" marriage) Ghana* [2006] UKAIT 00046 — `verify`
- **Principle:** "Subsisting" means more than a formally valid marriage — the marital relationship must be
  continuing.

### Goudey (subsisting marriage – evidence) Sudan [2012] UKUT 00041 (IAC) — `verify`
- **Principle:** Evidence of telephone contact/communication can suffice; there is no requirement for
  particular types of evidence (e.g. letters) to prove a subsisting relationship. Context matters.
- **Use:** Couples separated by distance/conflict with limited documentary trails.

### Naz (subsisting marriage – standard of proof) Pakistan [2012] UKUT 00040 (IAC) — `verify`
- **Principle:** Standard is balance of probabilities; assess the relationship's substance on all evidence.

### Agho v SSHD [2015] EWCA Civ 1198 — `core` (EEA context)
- **Principle:** Where marriage of convenience is alleged, the legal burden is on the SSHD; once the SSHD
  raises reasonable suspicion, the evidential burden shifts to the applicant to address it.

### Rosa v SSHD [2016] EWCA Civ 14 — `core` (EEA context)
- **Principle:** Legal burden of proving a marriage of convenience lies on the SSHD.
- **Use for both:** Argued by analogy in Appendix FM sham-marriage allegations; check how the tribunal has
  treated the analogy recently (`verify`).

### Papajorgji (EEA spouse – marriage of convenience) Greece [2012] UKUT 00038 (IAC) — `verify`
- **Principle:** No burden on the applicant to prove a marriage is not one of convenience unless evidence
  raises the issue.

## C. Insurmountable obstacles / exceptional circumstances

### R (Agyarko) v SSHD; R (Ikuga) v SSHD [2017] UKSC 11 — `core`
- **Principle:** "Insurmountable obstacles" is a stringent test (very significant difficulties that couldn't
  be overcome or would cause very serious hardship). Where family life was formed while status was
  precarious, removal will be disproportionate only in **exceptional** circumstances = where refusal results
  in **unjustifiably harsh consequences**. Endorsed the Rules + guidance structure.
- **Use:** Set the bar honestly for clients; build cumulative, evidenced factors.

### Lal v SSHD [2019] EWCA Civ 1925 — `core`
- **Principle:** Two-stage EX.2 test; obstacles assessed practically and realistically; a partner's genuine
  subjective (e.g. medical/psychological) difficulty can qualify if objectively reasonable and evidenced.
- **Use:** Medical evidence of the partner's condition and its interaction with conditions abroad.

### KO (Nigeria) & Ors v SSHD [2018] UKSC 53 — `core`
- **Principle:** "Reasonable to expect the child to leave" (s.117B(6)) is assessed in the real world —
  including where the parents are expected to be — but without blaming the child for parental misconduct.

### Zoumbas v SSHD [2013] UKSC 74 — `core`
- **Principle:** Best interests of the child are a primary (not paramount) consideration; must be assessed
  first and carefully.

### R (Razgar) v SSHD [2004] UKHL 27 — `core`
- **Principle:** Five-stage Article 8 structure (existence → interference → lawful → legitimate aim → proportionality).

### Huang v SSHD [2007] UKHL 11 — `core`
- **Principle:** Proportionality is for the decision-maker/tribunal to determine, striking a fair balance.

### Hesham Ali v SSHD [2016] UKSC 60 — `core`
- **Principle:** "Balance sheet" approach to Art 8 proportionality (deportation case, applied widely).

### TZ (Pakistan) & PG (India) v SSHD [2018] EWCA Civ 1109 — `core`
- **Principle:** Where an applicant meets the Immigration Rules, that is positive and normally determinative
  of the Art 8 appeal in their favour.
- **Use:** Appeals where the caseworker wrongly found a requirement unmet — show the rules are met.

### OA & Ors (human rights; 'new matter'; s.120) Nigeria [2019] UKUT 00065 (IAC) — `verify`
- **Principle:** Ability to satisfy the rules is relevant/weighty in Art 8 appeals; also addresses new matters.

### Miah v SSHD [2012] EWCA Civ 261 — `core`; Patel v SSHD [2013] UKSC 72 — `core`
- **Principle:** No "near-miss" principle; Art 8 is not a general dispensing power from the rules.

## D. Chikwamba (return-to-apply)

### Chikwamba v SSHD [2008] UKHL 40 — `core`
- **Principle:** Insisting on return abroad to apply for entry clearance may be disproportionate where it
  is a mere procedural formality, especially with children.

### R (Chen) v SSHD (Appendix FM – Chikwamba – temporary separation – proportionality) IJR [2015] UKUT 00189 (IAC) — `verify`
- **Principle:** The applicant must show that temporary separation would interfere disproportionately;
  evidence required, not assumption.

### Younas (section 117B(6)(b); Chikwamba; Zambrano) Pakistan [2020] UKUT 00129 (IAC) — `verify`
- **Principle:** Chikwamba applies where entry clearance would certainly be granted; considered within s.117B.

### Alam & Anor v SSHD [2023] EWCA Civ 30 — `verify`
- **Principle:** The Chikwamba principle has limited scope post-Part 5A; the question is overall Art 8
  proportionality, and failing the rules (e.g. financial evidence) counts in the public interest.

## E. Deception / suitability

### AA (Nigeria) v SSHD [2010] EWCA Civ 773 — `core`
- **Principle:** "False representations" in refusal grounds requires dishonesty, not innocent mistake.

### Ahmed (general grounds of refusal – material non-disclosure) Pakistan [2011] UKUT 00351 (IAC) — `verify`
- **Principle:** Non-disclosure of material facts as a refusal ground — dishonesty considerations. (Check
  exact citation & holding; several "Ahmed" UT decisions exist.)

## F. Entry clearance & private life

### Ali v SSHD (Handule) [2024] EWCA Civ 372 — `verify`
- **Principle (reported):** Refusal of entry clearance can engage a person's pre-existing UK private life. Check facts and scope.
