# Article 8 & Exceptions — when the rules aren't fully met

> Rules: Appendix FM **EX.1, EX.2, GEN.3.1, GEN.3.2, GEN.3.3**; statute: **NIAA 2002 Part 5A (s.117A–D)**;
> **s.55 BCIA 2009**; ECHR **Art. 8**. Guidance: *Family life (as a partner or parent), private life and
> exceptional circumstances*. Outcome if successful: usually **10-year route**.

## EX.1 — in-UK applications only (NOT entry clearance)
Applies where the applicant is in the UK and fails the **immigration status** requirement (e.g. visitor,
overstayer) or some finance/English requirements at leave-to-remain stage (check current wording).
- **EX.1(a) — child:** genuine and subsisting parental relationship with a child under 18 in the UK who is
  British **or** has lived in the UK continuously ≥ 7 years, **and** it would not be reasonable to expect
  the child to leave the UK (*KO (Nigeria) [2018] UKSC 53* — reasonableness assessed in the real world,
  without blaming the child for the parent's conduct).
- **EX.1(b) — partner:** genuine and subsisting relationship with a partner who is British/settled/refugee/HP,
  **and** there are **insurmountable obstacles** to family life continuing **outside the UK**.

### EX.2 — "insurmountable obstacles"
*"The very significant difficulties which would be faced by the applicant or their partner in continuing
their family life together outside the UK and which could not be overcome or would entail very serious
hardship for the applicant or their partner."*

**Two-stage test (Lal [2019] EWCA Civ 1925):** (1) is there a very significant difficulty? (2) could it be
overcome, or would it entail very serious hardship? Assessed practically and realistically; subjective
impact counts if genuine and objectively reasonable.

**What usually works (with strong evidence):** serious medical condition of partner requiring UK
treatment unavailable abroad; partner's caring responsibilities in UK (e.g. disabled child/parent from
another relationship, with shared custody/contact orders); real danger in applicant's country to partner
(e.g. same-sex couple in a criminalising country, religion, ethnicity); legal bar on partner entering/living
in applicant's country.
**What usually fails:** language barriers, partner's job/career, cost, "never been there", general
hardship, preference to live in UK (*Agyarko*).

## GEN.3.2 — exceptional circumstances (all applications incl. entry clearance)
Where the rules aren't met, grant if refusal would result in **unjustifiably harsh consequences** for the
applicant, partner, a relevant child or another family member whose Art 8 rights are affected.
Considers: best interests of children (primary consideration — s.55, *Zoumbas [2013] UKSC 74*), strength
of family life, cumulative factors, the public interest under s.117B.

## GEN.3.1 — alternative sources of money
Where the financial requirement isn't met **and** GEN.3.2 consequences would be unjustifiably harsh,
consider third-party support, applicant's prospective earnings, other credible sources (*MM (Lebanon)*).

## GEN.3.3 — best interests of children
Must be taken into account in every GEN.3.1/3.2 assessment.

## Section 117B public-interest considerations (tribunals MUST apply)
| s.117B | Point | Practical effect |
|---|---|---|
| (1) | Maintenance of effective immigration control is in the public interest | Weight against |
| (2) | Speaks English → less burden, better integrated | Show English ability |
| (3) | Financially independent → not a burden on taxpayers | Show finances (*Rhuppiah [2018] UKSC 58*) |
| (4) | **Little weight** to private life / relationship with qualifying partner formed when in UK **unlawfully** | Big problem for overstayers |
| (5) | Little weight to private life formed when status **precarious** (any leave short of ILR) | |
| (6) | Where person has genuine & subsisting parental relationship with a **qualifying child** (British / 7 yrs) and it is not reasonable to expect the child to leave → public interest does **not** require removal | Strong argument with British children |

## Chikwamba — "just go back and apply properly?"
*Chikwamba [2008] UKHL 40*: requiring someone to leave to apply from abroad, purely as a procedural
formality, may be disproportionate (especially with children). Narrowed since: *Agyarko*;
*Younas [2020] UKUT 129*; *Alam & Anor [2023] EWCA Civ 30* — the question is always overall Art 8
proportionality through s.117B; Chikwamba carries real weight mainly where entry clearance would
**certainly** be granted (e.g. all requirements met except status) and temporary separation would be
disproportionate.

## Article 8 structure (Razgar [2004] UKHL 27)
1. Family/private life exists? 2. Interference of sufficient gravity? 3. In accordance with law?
4. Legitimate aim? 5. **Proportionate?** — balance sheet (*Hesham Ali [2016] UKSC 60*), with s.117B,
and great weight on whether the rules are met (*TZ (Pakistan) [2018] EWCA Civ 1109*: if the rules are met,
refusal will normally be disproportionate).

**No "near-miss" principle:** narrowly missing a rule is not itself a reason to grant (*Miah [2012] EWCA Civ 261*;
*Patel [2013] UKSC 72*) — but how close the miss is can be relevant to proportionality.
